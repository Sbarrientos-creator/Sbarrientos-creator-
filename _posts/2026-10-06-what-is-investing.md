## The basic idea

**Saving** means keeping money safe so you can use it later. **Investing** means putting
money into something, such as a company or a fund, hoping it grows over time. The trade-off
is that investing can grow your money more than saving, but you can also lose some of it.

## Key words

- **Stock:** a small piece of ownership in a company.
- **Bond:** a loan you give to a company or government, which pays you interest.
- **ETF / fund:** a basket of many investments bought together.
- **Risk:** the chance that an investment loses value.
- **Inflation:** prices rising over time, which makes the same money buy less.

## The power of compound interest

Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. Imagine, as a made-up
example, that $100 grows 7% every year:

| Year | Balance |
|---|---|
| 0 | $100.00 |
| 5 | $140.26 |
| 10 | $196.72 |
| 20 | $386.97 |

The longer money has to grow, the bigger the effect. That is why starting early matters.

## Quick rule of thumb

The **Rule of 72** estimates how long money takes to double: divide 72 by the yearly rate.
At 7%, that is about 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10 years.

## Be careful

- Be suspicious of anyone promising guaranteed or "fast" profits. That is a classic scam sign.
- Past results do not guarantee future results.
- Never invest money you cannot afford to lose, and talk to a parent or guardian first.

## Practice safely

Many websites offer free **paper trading** (simulated investing with fake money). It is a great way
to learn how markets move without risking anything.
