*Educational content only. This is not financial advice.*

Young people are often targeted by online scams because they use social media a lot and
may be new to handling money. Knowing the warning signs is one of the best protections you have.

## 1. Guaranteed or very high returns

Real investments always carry risk. If someone promises "guaranteed profits" or "double your
money in a week", it is almost certainly a scam.

## 2. Pressure to act fast

Scammers say things like "only 2 spots left" or "offer ends today" so you do not have time to think
or ask anyone. A real opportunity will still be there tomorrow.

## 3. Asking for secrecy or personal data

Be careful if someone asks you to keep it secret from your family, or asks for your passwords,
verification codes, ID numbers or bank details. Legitimate companies do not ask for these in chat messages.

## 4. Strange payment methods

Requests to pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency transfers to unknown people or money apps to strangers
are a red flag, because this money is very hard to recover.

## 5. Influencers or strangers promising "easy money"

A flashy lifestyle on social media does not prove someone is an expert or honest. Many
"get-rich-quick" accounts are selling courses or looking for victims.

## What to do

- Stop and do not send money or information.
- Talk to a parent, guardian or teacher.
- Search the company's name together with the word "scam" or "reviews".
- Report suspicious accounts on the platform where you found them.
- Remember: if it sounds too good to be true, it probably is.
